# 1212. Deleting a client, resource server, principal or tenant removes what it owns

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-22
- **Deciders:** NCode Group

## Context

Every foreign key of the persistence model is `Restrict`, so a store that removes a row must remove or check every row
that references it. A client has secrets, grants to resource servers, consents, role assignments at its node and issued
grants; a dynamically registered client is also referenced by the grant of its registration access token. A tenant is
referenced by sessions and federated principals, while local accounts reference their tenant by identifier and have no
foreign key. A resource server and a principal are referenced by role assignments, and a principal's identifier is
generated by the server and is global, so it can hold assignments at the nodes of other tenants.

The Entity Framework InMemory provider enforces no foreign key, so a test of the hermetic host cannot see a store that
leaves a dependent behind (issue [#250](https://github.com/NCodeGroup/identity/issues/250)).

## Decision

**Deleting an owner removes what it owns, in the same unit of work.**

1. **A client is removed with everything that exists for it.** Deleting a client removes its secrets with their key
   material, its grants to resource servers, its consents, every role assignment at its resource node, ownership
   included, and every grant issued to it: its authorization codes, tokens, pushed authorization requests, device and
   CIBA grants, and its registration access token. Only authorization blocks the delete.
2. **A tenant is refused while it holds an operator's records, and is otherwise removed with everything it owns.** A
   tenant that holds a client or a non-system resource server is refused with `409 Conflict`
   ([ADR-1169](1169-every-tenant-is-provisioned-with-a-persisted-key-for-each-configured-algorithm-it-uses.md)). Everything else the tenant owns is removed in
   the same unit of work, the data of its end users included: local accounts and their claims, federated principals and
   identities, sessions, and grants. The usage ledger is kept
   ([ADR-1199](1199-tenant-usage-is-a-daily-ledger-that-replicas-add-to-once-and-prune-in-batches.md)), and the
   identifier and the issuer of the tenant are retired
   ([ADR-1160](1160-a-tenant-is-named-by-its-creator-validated-by-its-strategy-and-never-reissued.md)).
3. **The tenant store erases every tenant-owned entity.** The Entity Framework `TenantStore` removes every row of every
   model entity that implements `ISupportTenantEntity` whose `NormalizedTenantId` is the tenant's, and the
   `SecretEntity` rows that those rows reference through `ISupportSecretEntity`. The set of entities is read from the
   model, as the tenant query filter reads it
   ([ADR-1018](1018-tenant-selection-is-shared-and-tenant-bound-rows-are-scoped-at-the-persistence-layer.md)), so an
   entity that another package contributes, such as a local account, is erased with its tenant without a change to the
   store.
4. **Rows are removed through the change tracker.** Entity Framework orders the deletes. Foreign keys stay `Restrict`,
   and the schema needs no cascade.
5. **A deregistration relies on the store alone.** `DELETE` of a dynamically registered client
   ([ADR-1192](1192-dynamic-client-registration-rides-the-client-and-grant-stores-authorized-by-single-use-initial-access-tokens.md))
   removes the client through `IClientStore.RemoveAsync`, which removes its secrets and its registration access token
   with it.
6. **A resource server is removed with its scopes, its secrets and every role assignment at its node.** The management
   API allows the deletion of a resource server
   ([ADR-1026](1026-resource-servers-own-scopes-settings-and-keys-and-decide-a-clients-scopes-and-token-audiences.md)).
   The stores find the assignments by the node's resource type, which is the persisted resource type
   (`OpenIdResourceTypes`), and by the normalized identifier in the resource's tenant, so the management delete and the
   deregistration of a dynamically registered client both remove them.
7. **A principal is removed with every role assignment it holds, in every tenant.**
   `FederatedPrincipalStore.RemoveAsync` removes the role assignments whose normalized principal identifier is the
   principal's, in every tenant, because the identifier of a principal is global. `TenantStore.RemoveAsync` also removes
   the assignments that the principals of the deleted tenant hold in other tenants. A principal is also removed with
   its consents and grants in its tenant, after the termination of its sessions
   ([ADR-1229](1229-a-blocked-disabled-or-deleted-end-user-loses-its-sessions-and-is-refused-with-access-denied.md)).
8. **The last owner of a resource can be deleted.** Deletion is erasure, and the resource stays manageable through roles
   at the nodes of its ancestors.

## Options considered

- **Report the data of the end users as dependents (`409`).** Rejected: no API removes the users or the sessions of a
  tenant in bulk, and the data of a deleted tenant is unreachable because its identifier is retired, so keeping it is a
  privacy liability (GDPR erasure).
- **Keep the data detached.** Rejected: it leaves orphaned personal data with no owner.
- **Database cascades.** Rejected: SQL Server refuses multiple cascade paths, the Entity Framework InMemory provider
  cascades only the rows that it tracks, and local accounts have no foreign key.
- **A confirmation parameter on the delete.** Rejected: the `409` for operator-created records and the confirmation of
  the console already guard it.
- **Refuse a client while it has secrets.** Rejected: the secrets of a client exist only for it, as the keys of a tenant
  do ([ADR-1169](1169-every-tenant-is-provisioned-with-a-persisted-key-for-each-configured-algorithm-it-uses.md)), and Auth0 and Keycloak delete them with it.
- **Remove what the owner owns in the stores' own unit of work (chosen).** The deleting store removes every dependent
  through the change tracker, so a delete either removes the owner with everything it owns or fails whole.

## Consequences

- Deleting a client ends every refresh token and every reference grant issued to it at once. The JWT access tokens that
  were issued run to their expiry.
- Deleting a tenant erases its users irreversibly.
- A deleted client or resource server leaves no role assignment at its node.
- A deleted principal leaves no role assignment in any tenant, and no consent or grant.
- A contributed tenant-owned entity is erased with its tenant without a change to the store.
- The store loads the grants that it removes into one unit of work; ended grants are pruned after their retention
  ([ADR-1218](1218-ended-grants-are-removed-after-a-retention-period-in-bounded-batches.md)), which bounds it.
- The InMemory provider enforces no foreign key, so the store tests assert each dependent's absence instead of relying
  on a constraint.
